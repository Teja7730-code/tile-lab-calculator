Inter-Laboratory Comparison (ILC) Plan

Raw material & finished goods testing — proficiency & consistency programme (4 laboratories: SVPL Central Lab, SIPL Maliya, SIPL Tirupati, Nexion).

1. Purpose & scope

Monitor testing proficiency and result consistency across Simpolo group laboratories by comparing results on common blind-coded raw material samples against the assigned (standard) result, covering the full Master Template parameter set.

3. Testing frequency

RoundMonthly — sample sent on the 5th of every month
CoversAll applicable parameters: physical, chemical / firing, qualitative
Submission deadline7 working days

One combined scorecard is issued per round.

2. Sampling calendar

The laboratory that prepares and splits the sample rotates monthly.

2. Process

  1. The sampling laboratory selects a homogeneous sample, splits it into 4 blind-coded portions and sends one to each laboratory on the 5th of the month.
  2. Each laboratory tests its portion as per the applicable SOP.
  3. Each laboratory submits results to SVPL Central Lab on the Master scorecard within the deadline.
  4. SVPL Central Lab compares results, flags deviations and issues the round scorecard.
  5. Deviations trigger corrective action; records are retained for 3 years.
  6. The designated Quality Manager (SVPL) coordinates sample preparation, report collection, assessment, Z-score reporting and corrective / preventive actions.
  7. The final assessment of all 4 laboratories is shared with the heads of the respective units.

Sample requirements

Raw material3 kg each: high / semi / low plasticity clay, soda & potash feldspar, wollastonite, bentonite, quartz, talc, zirconium silicate
Tiles 600 × 1200 mm20 tiles per sample
Tiles 1200 × 1800 mm & above5 tiles per sample

4. Result submission

Scorecard format: material name, material code, blind sample code, sample receiver & date, all parameter values, tester / reviewer names.

5. Performance evaluation criteria

Z = (Laboratory result − Assigned value) / Standard deviation
|Z| ≤ 2Satisfactory
2 < |Z| < 3Warning
|Z| ≥ 3Unsatisfactory

Warning and unsatisfactory results are reviewed and corrective action is taken where required. Missed or late submission is a non-conformance. See Z-score method for how the assigned value and SD are derived.

7. Non-conformance & corrective action

  1. “Attention Required” results are reviewed jointly by the Central Lab Coordinator and the concerned lab within 5 working days; root cause and corrective action are tracked to closure.
  2. Repeated “Attention Required” status (2 consecutive months, same parameter) triggers a focused re-test and, if needed, calibration / proficiency retraining.
  3. All submissions, scorecards and corrective action records are retained by SVPL Central Lab for at least 3 years.

How the Z-score is calculated

Based on the ILC Plan formula and the NIST Engineering Statistics Handbook, section 1.3.5.17 (Detection of Outliers; Iglewicz & Hoaglin modified Z-score).

Assigned value X̃ = median of the laboratory results
Scale (SD) σ̂ = 1.4826 × MAD, where MAD = median of |xᵢ − X̃|
Z-score Z = (xᵢ − X̃) / σ̂ = 0.6745 (xᵢ − X̃) / MAD
|Z| ≤ 2Satisfactory
2 < |Z| < 3Warning — review
|Z| ≥ 3Unsatisfactory — root-cause review

Why median and MAD for 4 laboratories

  1. With n = 4, the largest Z-score possible from the group’s own mean and sample SD is (n − 1)/√n = 1.5. No laboratory could ever reach |Z| > 2, so nothing would ever be flagged.
  2. The median and MAD are not pulled by one bad result, so a deviating laboratory stands out instead of hiding itself.
  3. The plan’s “Standard” column on the scorecard is this assigned value.

Rules applied by this site

  1. At least 3 laboratory results are needed to score a parameter; fewer is shown as Incomplete.
  2. If MAD = 0 (results tie), σ̂ = 1.253314 × mean absolute deviation (Iglewicz & Hoaglin). If all results are equal, Z = 0.
  3. |Z| > 3.5 is labelled a potential outlier (NIST). Outliers are investigated, never deleted automatically.
  4. Qualitative parameters are compared for agreement; a differing observation is flagged as Variation.
  5. The method assumes roughly normal data; check unusual patterns before drawing conclusions.
